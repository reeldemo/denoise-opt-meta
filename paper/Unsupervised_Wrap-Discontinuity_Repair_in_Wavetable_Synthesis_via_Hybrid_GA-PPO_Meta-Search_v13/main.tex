  \maketitle
  \begin{center}\footnotesize
    Code (ReelSynth engine + DenoiseOpt meta-search):
    \url{https://github.com/reeldemo/reelsynth},
    \url{https://github.com/reeldemo/denoise-opt-meta}
  \end{center}
  \begin{abstract}
    Mismatched wavetable endpoints leave a wrap-join click at the seam on every period.
    We study unsupervised repair of that local discontinuity: search never sees paired studio-clean targets, and scoring uses a procedural cliff-withheld ideal sibling (self-supervised geometry) plus mid-cycle identity to the cracked engine.
    Classical raised-cosine fades, virtual-analog seam operators, and corrupt-to-corrupt Noise2Noise are strong baselines on some boards.
    Transfer benches reuse the same wrap-discontinuity protocol on other periodic signals as stress tests, not domain diagnosis claims.
    Because no single operator fits every engine family, \textbf{DenoiseOpt} applies a hybrid genetic-algorithm (GA), proximal policy optimization (PPO), and population-based training (PBT) meta-loop to search compact repair operators, ranking candidates by the blended residual $R_{\mathrm{blend}}$.
    This is an application of known search tools to a crisp DSP problem, not a new learning algorithm.
    A $1{,}200$-iteration hybrid champion reaches about $0.9697$ on holdout $R_{\mathrm{blend}}$, below frozen Noise2Noise (about $0.9750$) and well above Dual Cosine (about $0.541$).
    On twenty multi-family boards, Ours edges Noise2Noise on mean score, and both beat Dual Cosine.
    On hard wrap cliffs, classical fades collapse while searched operators hold.
    We do not claim wrap-closure proofs, search-convergence theorems, or speech/music SOTA.
  \end{abstract}
  \keywords{wavetable synthesis; wrap discontinuity; unsupervised repair; DenoiseOpt; seam restoration; Noise2Noise; neural architecture search; reinforcement learning; genetic algorithms}
  \vspace{0.6em}
}]
