\section{Transfer pilot on CWRU bearings and MIT-BIH ECG}
\label{app:transfer-pilot}

\paragraph{Classical-board disclaimer (read first).}
This appendix is a \emph{classical-board} transfer pilot only.
We compare the winning outer loop against no-bake, DualCosine, fades, endpoint pin, seam FIR, and domain classical proxies.
We did \emph{not} run published deep learning SOTA on these domains (no Cycle-GAN, BeatDiff, MFPT/Paderborn deep campaign, PTB-XL full, power-systems, CNC, or formal clinical denoisers under this protocol).
Do not read Table~\ref{tab:transfer-pilot} as a deep-SOTA claim.

\paragraph{What was run.}
We transferred the winning outer loop (\textbf{Ours / hybrid GA--PPO}, approach code \texttt{hybrid\_lstm})
to two public cycle-local wrap tasks outside musical wavetables:
Case Western Reserve University (CWRU) bearing acceleration periods and MIT-BIH ECG beat windows
($L{=}256$; prolonged residual $R$ vs an ideal sibling from domain-specific periodization).
Outer-loop budget is modest ($250$ outer iters per dataset, \texttt{fit\_steps}${}={}40$).
This is a pilot of a few hours, not a multi-day domain campaign.
Real content is z-scored per period; absolute clinical or engineering scale is not preserved.
Artifacts: \texttt{reelsynth/brand/artifacts/signal\_heal\_transfer/}.
Prose source: \texttt{denoise-opt-meta/docs/SIGNAL\_HEAL\_TRANSFER\_PILOT.md}.

\paragraph{Reading the numbers.}
On CWRU, classical DualCosine and fades hurt versus no-bake.
Ours only narrowly beats no-bake on holdout.
Transfer is weak on that wrap construction.
On MIT-BIH ECG, DualCosine collapses morphology while Ours leads the classical board.
Endpoint pin is the strongest classical comparator there.
Primary paper claims remain cycle-local \emph{wavetable} seam restoration.
