\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Main story under $R_{\mathrm{blend}}$.}
The locked protocol does not crown the hybrid champion over Noise2Noise on the holdout
($0.9697$ vs $0.9750$).
Dual Cosine on that board sits near $0.541$.
The useful reading is therefore not ``searched bake beats every neural baseline.''
It is: compact searchable repair operators that outperform classical fades, stay competitive with Noise2Noise on multi-family means ($13/20$ wins), and hold on hard cliffs where Dual Cosine collapses
(Table~\ref{tab:n2n-vs-ours}, Section~\ref{sec:cliff-strata}).
Older whole-curve numbers near $R{\approx}0.991$ belong in the historical boards only (Limitations).

\paragraph{Which families stay hard.}
Across large procedural benches, wrap error is uneven.
Families such as \texttt{nonlinear}, \texttt{combo}, \texttt{triple\_mix}, and \texttt{extreme\_overlay} dominate the residual budget; \texttt{harmonic\_fft} and \texttt{am\_fm} are easier.
Mean scores hide that structure.

\paragraph{Search near the ceiling.}
Under near-ceiling residuals, further gains are small in absolute units and sensitive to reward shaping and branch weights.
Plateau adaptation (deeper operators, denser MoE graphs, higher GA/PBT weight) is the operational response inside one campaign.
Matched outer-loop bake-offs and HP probes sit in Appendix~\ref{app:supplement}.

\paragraph{Evidence beyond static tiles.}
Vibrato spectrograms and downloadable hear WAVs support informal inspection (Sections~\ref{sec:vibrato-eval}--\ref{sec:hear-samples}).
Sci/eng wrap transfer is a classical-board stress test (Section~\ref{sec:transfer-main}).
Perceptual and scope limits are collected in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
