\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

DenoiseOpt casts wavetable wrap discontinuity as cycle-local seam repair under a blended residual
$R_{\mathrm{blend}}$ and latency-aware objective $J$, with a hybrid GA--PPO(+PBT) outer loop over bake cells
(including searchable SeamN2N-parity \texttt{n2n\_unet}).
Corrupt$\to$corrupt Noise2Noise is the primary neural baseline and champ gate
(frozen holdout $R_{\mathrm{blend}}{\approx}0.975$); Dual Cosine is the classical fade comparator
(holdout $R_{\mathrm{blend}}{\approx}0.541$).
A $1{,}200$-iteration hybrid freeze reaches $R_{\mathrm{blend}}{\approx}0.9695$ ($J{\approx}0.960$) but does not clear the N2N gate,
while clearly beating Dual Cosine.
On multi-family boards Ours wins $13/20$ mean comparisons vs N2N; N2N leads holdout and Factory+FX/AKWF
(${\approx}1{,}280$-cycle balanced corpora), and both beat Dual Cosine on those boards.
Named sci/eng wrap transfer (CWRU, MFPT $n{=}256$, MIT-BIH, PTB-XL 500\,Hz subset, synth CNC/PMU)
shows Ours leading classical boards---including Dual Cosine---under prolonged $R$, with measured CUDA latency;
domain-trained N2N quality was not executed there.
Deep SOTA weights and formal MOS were not run.
Scope remains wavetable seam restoration for DSP / synthesis venues.
